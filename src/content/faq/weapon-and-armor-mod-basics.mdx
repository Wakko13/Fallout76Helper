## Not the same system as legendary effects

This entry covers **standard mods** — the physical parts you attach to a weapon or armor piece (scopes,
receivers, linings, etc.). It's a completely different system from **legendary star-tier effects** (the
1-3 star random effects like "Bloodied" or "Anti-Armor" rolled with Legendary Modules/Scrip) — see
[Legendary effects & crafting](/reference/legendary-effects-overview) for that system. Every weapon and armor
piece has both: its standard mods determine its base stats, and if it's legendary, star effects stack on top.

## Weapon mod slots (attach points)

Weapons workbenches let you build and swap mods into fixed slots per weapon — the game calls each one an
**attach point**. Depending on the weapon type, these typically include:

- **Receiver** — usually the biggest stat swing (damage type/output, sometimes changes the weapon's fire mode).
- **Barrel** — affects range, accuracy, sometimes weight.
- **Sights/Scopes** — from iron sights up through long, night-vision, or reflex scopes; cosmetic-ish but
  affects zoom and sometimes hip-fire accuracy.
- **Stock** — sighted accuracy and, on some weapons, bash damage.
- **Magazine** — ammo capacity and reload speed.
- **Grip** — recoil control and hip-fire accuracy.
- **Muzzle** (compensators, brakes, suppressors) — recoil, noise (stealth), or bonus damage tradeoffs.

Melee weapons use a smaller equivalent set — grips, materials, and head/blade attachments instead of
barrels/scopes.

Crafting a new mod at the workbench **directly replaces** whatever's currently in that slot — there's no need
to remove the old one first, and the material cost of the old mod isn't refunded.

## Where mod plans come from

You need to actually know a mod (via a learned plan) before the workbench lets you craft it — having materials
alone isn't enough:

- **Finding plans** as loot — world containers, quest/event rewards, vendors (including Gold Bullion vendors
  and faction vendors for some higher-tier mods).
- **Scrapping weapons** at any workbench has a chance to teach you a random mod used on that weapon — this is
  the main way most players fill out their mod library over time, and it's RNG per scrap, not guaranteed.
- Simply **looting or buying a weapon** that already has a mod you don't know doesn't teach you that mod by
  itself — you have to scrap it (or a duplicate) to learn it.
- The **Gunsmith perk** is required to modify (and repair) guns above a certain tier at all, and higher ranks
  unlock crafting the higher-tier mods/receivers; **Science!** gates some energy-weapon mods similarly.

## Armor mod slots

Armor pieces (and underarmor specifically) have their own, smaller mod system at the **Armor Workbench**:

- Standard armor pieces typically get one **misc mod slot** — things like **Pocketed**/**Deep Pocketed**
  (carry weight), or resistance-type mods, depending on armor type.
- **Underarmor** carries its own separate mod slots (commonly a **lining** slot plus a second slot added in a
  later rework) — linings no longer give a flat universal bonus across all underarmor; their effect depends on
  which underarmor piece they're applied to.
- Same rule as weapons: you need the plan for a specific armor mod (piece + mod combo, e.g. "Pocketed combat
  armor chest") before you can craft it, and plans come from the same sources — loot, vendors, and scrapping
  armor pieces you find.

## Uncertain / worth double-checking

The exact list of attach points varies meaningfully by weapon archetype (a pistol and a heavy gun don't share
identical slot types), and the underarmor mod-slot rework has changed more than once across patches — treat the
"lining + second slot" description as current but verify against a fresh source if a specific underarmor
piece's slots matter for a build.

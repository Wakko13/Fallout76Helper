## Claiming a workshop

There are **22 workshops** scattered across Appalachia, each marked by its own large red workbench and bounded
by a green perimeter line (same visual as a CAMP boundary). To claim one:

1. Approach an unclaimed workshop and interact with its workbench to start the **Claim Workshop** event —
   waves of enemies spawn and you (or your team) need to clear them.
2. Pay a **caps fee** to complete the claim. The cost scales with how built-up the workshop already is —
   claiming a bare/reset workshop is cheap (roughly 20-100+ caps), while claiming one that's already been
   fought over and built up costs noticeably more.
3. Once claimed, you automatically unlock crafting plans for the resource extractors matching whatever deposits
   that workshop sits on (ore, wood, fiber, etc.) — these plans can then be built at that workshop **or** at
   your CAMP if your CAMP happens to be placed next to a matching resource deposit.

## Defending it (and losing it to other players)

Claiming a workshop flags you for PvP around it specifically:

- Periodically, a **Defend Workshop** event triggers — you get a short window (sometimes cited as ~90 seconds)
  to return and prep before waves of enemies attack the workshop's objectives. This part doesn't involve other
  players; it's a PvE holdout.
- Separately, **any other player can walk up and start reclaiming your workshop** by interacting with the
  workbench, which triggers the **Attack on Workshop** quest and flags both of you for PvP at that location —
  you take full damage from each other even on a normally non-PvP server, but only in that immediate area.
  While you still hold the workshop, any turrets you've built defend it against the attacker; if the workshop
  flips to neutral/theirs mid-fight, those same turrets turn on you.
- If you lose the fight (or just don't show up), the attacker claims it and the quest stays active — you can
  pay to reclaim it later, or the workshop becomes free again automatically if the new owner disconnects.

## Resource extractors

Extractors are the payoff for holding a workshop:

- They only produce if built on/near the specific resource deposit a workshop has (e.g. lead, aluminum, oil,
  wood — varies per workshop; check a location's resource icons before claiming if you want a specific
  material).
- Extractors need **100 electricity** to run, supplied either by a powered-up power plant (if that workshop's
  "Powering Up" event has been completed on the server) or by generators you build yourself.
- Yield ticks on a timer per deposit rather than continuously — commonly cited around every ~27-45 minutes
  depending on the resource, collected from the extractor's output.
- Extractors can be damaged or destroyed during Defend Workshop waves or an Attack on Workshop fight, and stop
  producing until repaired.

## "Maintenance," decay, and losing ownership passively

There's no ongoing caps tax or upkeep fee for holding a workshop once claimed — the real "maintenance" cost is
that ownership isn't persistent:

- **Logging out drops your claim.** Workshops are tied to your play session on that server, not saved
  permanently to your character — if you log off, the workshop goes unclaimed (built structures generally
  remain standing for a while, but any items stored in its containers are lost once you leave the session).
- **Server restarts reset workshop ownership** the same way.
- Reconnecting quickly to the same server sometimes preserves your claim for a short grace window, but this
  isn't guaranteed and shouldn't be relied on.

Because of this, workshops function more as a **temporary, session-length resource farm** than a long-term base
— most players claim one right before an extended farming session rather than treating ownership as permanent.

## Uncertain / worth double-checking

Exact caps costs to claim/reclaim, extractor tick timers, and the ~90-second defend-prep window are consistently
reported by community guides but not pinned down in official Bethesda documentation, and may vary by workshop
size or have shifted across patches — verify against a current guide if precision matters (e.g. deciding whether
a specific workshop's extractor cycle is worth camping for).

## Current stash weight limit

**1,200 lbs**, shared across everything you put in your stash box (weapons, armor,
aid, junk — all of it counts against the same total). This is the number every
current guide and the wiki list as of this writing.

Flagging this clearly per the nature of the number: the limit has been raised
several times over the game's life (400 → 600 → 800 → 1,200 lbs, the last bump
landing in a January 2021 patch), and Bethesda has openly discussed wanting to
raise it further in response to constant player requests. I could not find any
patch notes confirming an increase past 1,200 lbs as of this writing — if you see
a different number in-game, trust what the Pip-Boy shows you over this article,
since this is exactly the kind of figure that changes with updates.

## The separate scrap problem

Raw junk (pre-scrapped items) counts toward the same 1,200 lb stash cap as
everything else — there's no separate "junk-only" limit in the base game, which is
why junk hoarders hit the wall fastest. Scrapping junk into components (screws,
springs, adhesive, etc.) doesn't reduce weight either; components have their own
weight per unit and a huge pile of aluminum or steel can weigh just as much as the
junk it came from.

## What actually helps

- **Fallout 1st's Scrapbox** is the real solution, not a workaround. It's a
  second, separate storage container exclusively for scrapped components (not
  whole junk items) with **no weight limit** — effectively unlimited component
  storage as long as you're subscribed. This is the single biggest stash-weight
  fix in the game.
- **Scrap Kits** (a Fallout 1st perk) are consumables that scrap every junk item
  in your inventory in one click and send the resulting components straight to
  your Scrapbox instead of your regular stash — meaning junk collected in the
  field never touches your 1,200 lb cap at all if you're subscribed.
- **Without Fallout 1st**, your main levers are: scrap junk regularly instead of
  hoarding whole items (some components are individually lighter than the item
  they came from), sell/vendor unneeded gear and aid frequently, and keep an eye
  on which components you're overstocked on (aluminum and steel pile up fastest
  for most builds) and dump or use them down.
- **Perk cards** like Pack Rat (junk items weigh less) and Bandolier (ammo weighs
  less) help your carried weight, but note they only affect what you're carrying,
  not your stash box total.
- There is no craftable or purchasable "extra stash box" outside Fallout 1st —
  everyone shares the same single 1,200 lb stash per character (not per world),
  including across all your CAMPs.

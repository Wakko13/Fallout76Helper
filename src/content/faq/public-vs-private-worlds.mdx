## Public worlds

This is the default game mode everyone plays without any subscription. Each public world/server holds **24 players** as a baseline, expandable up to **32** to keep teammates who queue together on the same instance. Every other player on your world is shown on your map at all times — there's no hidden-player design in Fallout 76. Which specific server you land on when you launch or fast-travel is effectively random; you don't pick one from a browser list.

## Private worlds (Fallout 1st only)

Private worlds are a **Fallout 1st subscription** perk, not something available to free players:

- The **owner** of a private world must be a Fallout 1st subscriber. Invited friends do **not** need their own subscription to join.
- Capacity is small: up to **7 invited friends plus the owner**, 8 people total.
- You create one from the main menu's Play screen (a dedicated Private Worlds / Custom Worlds option appears there for subscribers) and invite people in afterward through the in-game Social menu.
- The world stays up on-demand as long as **at least one Fallout 1st member** is present in it — it doesn't require the original owner specifically to stay online, just any subscriber. If every subscriber leaves, the world shuts down.
- Gameplay rules are otherwise identical to a public world (same quests, events, loot) unless you specifically set up a Custom World with adjusted settings — a private world isn't a cheat-mode sandbox by default, it's just a public-rules world with a locked guest list.
- Everything you'd expect still carries over normally: character progression, inventory, and stash are shared account-wide regardless of which world you're playing in.

Why people use them: performance (fewer players means less server strain, more consistent enemy AI/hit registration), practicing boss fights or building without interruption, or just playing solo/with friends without randoms wandering into your CAMP or contesting your workshop.

## World hopping

"World hopping" (also called server hopping) means deliberately quitting to the main menu and rejoining — since your next server is effectively randomly assigned, this usually drops you onto a **different server instance** with its own independent state. People do this to:

- Get a **fresh copy of a location** — respawned resource nodes, un-looted containers, an unclaimed workshop, or crashed vertibirds/events that already got cleared on your old server.
- Find a server where a **desired public event** is currently running or about to start, since events are per-server.
- Work around a server that's performing badly or is overcrowded at a location you want to farm.

It doesn't erase your own account-level pick-up history — the game tracks roughly your last 250+ looted items regardless of server, so items you've already picked up recently still won't reappear for you even on a new server until enough other pickups push them off that list. Hopping mainly helps with server-side state (who else already cleared the area, what events are live, whether a workshop is claimed), not your personal loot cooldowns.

## Frame (chassis) vs. armor pieces

Power armor in Fallout 76 is two separate things stacked together:

- **The chassis/frame** is the bare exoskeleton — a deployable world object you can find,
  place, and climb into. **The empty frame has no level requirement at all**; any
  character, even level 1, can enter and walk around in a bare chassis (you just get none
  of the armor's damage resistance until you attach pieces).
- **The six armor pieces** (helmet, chest, left/right arm, left/right leg) bolt onto the
  frame individually and each has its own level requirement. You can't equip a piece above
  your current level — the frame won't stop you from entering, but you won't be able to
  attach an over-leveled part to it.

You almost never find a complete matching set in the wild; most world spawns are frames
with two or three of six pieces attached. Filling the rest out means finding duplicates,
crafting replacements at a power armor station (needs the plan), or looting more frames.

## Entering and exiting

Walk up to the back of the frame and interact (the same "activate" button you use for
everything else) to climb in — the armor unfolds and seals around you. To get back out,
**hold** the same interact button rather than tapping it. If you're ever stuck unable to
exit, check that you're not overloaded/overencumbered — that can block exiting in some
cases — and see Bethesda's support page for other stuck-in-armor fixes.

## Fusion cores

Power armor needs a **fusion core** installed to do anything beyond act as heavy, slow
armor: without one you cannot sprint, power-attack, or fast travel while suited up, and
your movement speed suffers. A core drains continuously while worn and moving, faster
during sprinting or power attacks, and stops draining while you're standing still or the
armor is unequipped/stored.

Getting cores early on:
- **Loot them off other power armor** you find in the world — most world-spawn suits
  come with a core already at partial charge.
- **Scavenge fusion generators/reactors** found in the environment (nuclear plants,
  military sites) — many contain a lootable core.
- **Steamer trunks and ammo boxes** at military locations (Camp McClintock and similar)
  have a chance to contain one; the Scrounger perk improves your odds from ammo boxes.
- **Craft them at a chemistry station** once you have the recipe, using nuclear material
  and other components.
- For a renewable passive supply later on, a claimed workshop with a **fusion core
  processor** (Monongah Power Plant, Poseidon Energy Plant, Thunder Mountain Power Plant)
  can passively generate a fresh, fully-charged core roughly every 7-8 minutes once the
  site event is completed and powered.

## Getting your first suit

There's no single scripted "here's a free full suit" quest reward right at the start —
that's a common misconception. What actually happens: a bare, non-level-locked power
armor frame (usually a T-45 or T-51 chassis with a couple of pieces attached) is very
easy to stumble into within the first hour or two near Vault 76 (Morgantown Trainyard
and Camden Park are commonly cited early spots), so most new players end up in *some*
power armor almost immediately just by exploring. If you want a reliably strong early
set specifically, **Watoga Municipal Center** is well known among players for
consistently having a near-complete free T-45 or T-51 set to loot. Full, guaranteed
sets do come later as actual quest rewards — e.g. Raider power armor from "Key to the
Past" and Excavator power armor (mining/carry-weight bonuses) from the level-25
"Miner Miracles" questline.

## Repairing

**Power armor stations** (a placeable CAMP object, plan-gated, or found at some fixed
world locations) are required to repair damaged pieces, swap/craft new pieces, and apply
paint/mods to power armor — you can't repair power armor at a regular armor workbench.
Keep spare adhesive, screws, and springs on hand since power armor repairs eat through
them fast, especially on higher-level pieces.

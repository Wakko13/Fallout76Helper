For how mod slots and Weapons Workbenches work in general, see
[Weapon and armor mod basics](../../faq/weapon-and-armor-mod-basics/). For where the Gatling Gun
sits relative to the rest of the heavy-weapon family — including how it compares to the Minigun
it's most often weighed against — see [Heavy weapons](../heavy-weapons/).

The Gatling Gun has six mod slots — more than the Minigun's three — which lines up with it being
the more tunable, ammo-efficient of the two 5mm sustained-fire heavy guns. Materials below come
from Fextralife; effect percentages that Fextralife left blank are filled in from Nukes & Dragons
where available.

## Receiver mods

| Mod | Effect | Perk required | Materials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard Receiver | Standard | — | 2x Adhesive, 1x Gear, 1x Screw, 1x Oil, 3x Wood, 3x Steel |
| Speedy Receiver | Fire Rate +100, Weight +10% | Gunsmith 4 | 2x Adhesive, 1x Oil, 3x Screw, 3x Steel, 3x Wood |
| Prime Receiver | Damage to Scorched +65% | Not documented (likely plan-gated, matching the Minigun's equivalent Prime mod) | Not documented |

## Uncertain / worth double-checking

Fextralife's scrape listed identical materials for the Short and Long Barrel rows, which reads as
a likely table-duplication error rather than confirmed fact — the Long Barrel almost certainly
costs somewhat more, so treat that materials cell as provisional. The Receiver slot's Prime option
was only found via Nukes & Dragons (no Fextralife row at all) and its perk/materials are unconfirmed
anywhere checked. Scope-slot materials weren't documented on either source. As with the other
entries in this series, exact numeric values and crafting costs shift with balance patches — verify
against a live wiki or in-game before committing scarce materials.

This isn't an attempt to catalog Fallout 76's perk cards — there are hundreds, spread
across seven SPECIAL trees, and new ones ship with updates. For how the SPECIAL
point budget gates which cards (and what rank) you can actually equip, see
`special-overview.mdx`. This entry covers two things: how you acquire cards in the
first place, and a short "almost everyone wants these" list to get a new or mid-level
character functional before you start optimizing toward a specific build.

## How you get perk cards

Perk cards are **earned by leveling, not bought**. Every level-up lets you pick one
new perk card to add to your collection (alongside a SPECIAL point to spend). On top
of that, you receive a **Perk Card Pack** — 4 regular cards plus 1 guaranteed gold
(higher-rarity) card — at levels 4, 6, 8, and 10, then every 5 levels after that (15,
20, 25...), continuing past level 50. There's no cap-shop path to acquiring cards;
the only way to get more is to keep leveling.

Duplicate/unwanted cards you pull from packs aren't wasted — scrapping them at a
perk card recycler machine (found at most major settlements) converts them into
Perk Card Flux, used to craft or upgrade specific cards you actually want.

## Cards almost everyone wants early

These solve universal problems (inventory weight, staying alive, wasting crafting
materials) regardless of what build you're aiming for:

| Card | SPECIAL | What it fixes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong Back | Strength | Extra carry weight capacity, plus (at higher ranks) lets you fast-travel or sprint while overencumbered |
| Bandolier / Batteries Included | Strength | Cuts the carry weight of ballistic ammo / energy ammo — huge if you're constantly overweight |
| Traveling Pharmacy | Intelligence | Reduces the weight of Stimpacks, RadAway, Buffout, and other chems — lets you actually carry a full kit |
| Team Medic | Charisma | Sharing this means one player's Stimpack heals the whole nearby team — strong in any group content |
| Full Charge / Fireproof | Endurance | Passive regen and resistance while your character is otherwise idle (loading screens, standing near fire) |
| Super Duper | Intelligence | Chance to craft double output from a single recipe (ammo, chems, food) — compounds over a full playthrough |
| Pack Rat | (junk weight generally) | Some junk items become "weightless" below a threshold — cuts down the constant inventory management |

None of these lock you into an archetype — they're general quality-of-life, which is
why they're worth grabbing before you specialize.

## How build archetypes diverge from here

Once the basics are covered, most players start pulling cards toward one of a few
common archetypes. This is a starting map — each of these has entire dedicated
build guides on sites like FalloutBuilds, and cards get swapped constantly as new
seasons and balance changes land.

- **Bloodied / low-HP builds** — intentionally stay near-death (often paired with
  radiation exposure) to trigger damage multipliers from Bloodied-legendary weapons
  and Unyielding armor. Leans on Nerd Rage! (Intelligence — bonus damage/resistance
  and an AP refresh below 20% health), Radicool, and Ricochet-type survivability
  cards to make near-death sustainable instead of just risky.
- **Heavy gunner** — built around the Strength tree's Heavy Gunner damage cards,
  Stabilized (accuracy while firing from the hip or aiming with heavy weapons in
  power armor), and ammo-weight-reduction cards, since heavy weapons burn ammo fast.
- **Stealth commando** — combines Agility's sneak-detection-reduction cards with
  Covert Operative-style sneak-attack-damage cards on suppressed weapons; often
  paired with Luck-tree critical-hit cards for a "one shot from stealth" pattern.
- **Melee** — Strength's melee damage cards (e.g. Martial Artist / Iron Fist for
  unarmed, or the bladed/blunt equivalents) plus Agility cards that boost AP
  regeneration, since melee builds spend AP on VATS or power attacks constantly.

## Uncertain / worth double-checking

Exact card names, ranks, and numeric bonuses shift with balance patches more often
than most other systems in this game — treat the table above as "what to look for,"
not exact wording, and check FalloutBuilds' perk pages for the current numbers
before finalizing a build around a specific card.

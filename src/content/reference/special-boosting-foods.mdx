For the cooking station mechanic itself (how recipes are learned, why cooking matters at all), see
[cooking basics](../cooking-basics/). This entry is specifically about the subset of cooked dishes that grant a
temporary SPECIAL stat point — a popular trick before a tough boss fight or a min-maxed build swap, since a
free +2/+3 to a stat you're dumping perk cards into for the next 30-90 minutes is essentially free power.

**Important caveat before you stack a pile of these:** food buffs of the same category that touch the same
stat **don't stack or add together** — eating a second SPECIAL-boosting dish just overwrites whatever's left of
the first one's timer rather than combining them. You get one active food-based SPECIAL bonus at a time (though
it can stack with a *drink*-category or *chem*-category buff to the same stat, since those are separate
categories).

## Known SPECIAL-boosting dishes

| Dish | SPECIAL boosted | Amount | Recipe source | Other effects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deathclaw Wellington | Strength | +3 | Recipe found at Moonshiner's Shack | Restores 90 HP, -25% hunger, +5 rad |
| Carrot Soup | Perception | +2 | Default (learned on picking up Carrot) | Restores 12 HP, -15% thirst, -10% hunger, +5 rad |
| Roasted Scorchbeast Heart | Endurance | +3 | Default once you have Scorchbeast Heart | Restores 200 HP, -25% hunger, +10 rad |
| Aged Mirelurk Queen Steak | Endurance | +3 | Undocumented ("???" on community wikis) | Restores 90 HP, -25% hunger, +5 rad |
| Corn Pone | Charisma | +2 | Default (uses Corn, Mirelurk Egg, Sugar) | Restores 45 HP, -15% hunger, +5 rad |
| Brain Fungus Soup | Intelligence | +2 | Undocumented ("???" on community wikis) | Restores 12 HP, -15% thirst, -10% hunger, +5 rad |
| Mirelurk Softshell Cake | Agility | +3 | Undocumented ("???" on community wikis) | Restores 60 HP, -20% hunger, +2 rad |
| Iguana Soup | Luck | +3 | Default (uses Iguana Bits, Silt Bean) | Restores 60 HP, -15% thirst, -20% hunger, +2 rad |

These are the dishes independently verified against current Fextralife item pages for this entry. Fextralife's
broader Food page lists several more candidates at lower magnitude (Grilled Hermit Crab, Mutt Chops, Dog Meat
Steak, Cat Meat Steak, Crispy Squirrel Bits, Fox Jerky, Ground Mole Rat, Appalachili, Awesome Opossum Bacon,
Charred Scorchbeast Liver, Chew Stick, Iguana on a Stick) — worth checking that page directly if you're hunting
for a specific stat and don't have the ingredients for anything above.

Two names commonly repeated in community "best SPECIAL foods" round-ups — **Grilled Radstag** and **Canned Dog
Food** — turned out on verification to **not** grant any SPECIAL bonus (Grilled Radstag gives +20 Carry Weight
instead; Canned Dog Food is just flat HP/rad with no stat effect). If you've seen either listed as a SPECIAL
food elsewhere, that's a common mix-up worth correcting.

## Why this matters (the min-max use case)

The trick is timing: eat the dish right before a boss fight, Daily Op, or heavy crafting/repair session so the
temporary point lands where it counts — e.g. Deathclaw Wellington's +3 Strength for extra carry weight and melee
damage heading into a fight, or Brain Fungus Soup's +2 Intelligence to squeeze one more rank out of an
Intelligence-gated perk card setup for that session. Because the bonus is real SPECIAL (not just a flat damage
buff), it also nudges anything else that scales off that stat — carry weight off Strength, hacking off
Intelligence, AP regen off Agility, and so on.

## Uncertain / worth double-checking

This table reflects Fextralife's current listed values, spot-checked per-item at the time of writing — Bethesda
doesn't publish an official master list, and past patches have adjusted individual food effects without much
notice, so a specific number here could drift after a future update. Several recipe *origins* are marked
undocumented even by the wikis themselves ("???") — those dishes are real and their effects are verified, but
exactly where the Recipe item drops from is genuinely unknown community-wide, not just missing from this
summary. The no-stacking-within-category rule is consistently reported by players across multiple guides but
isn't spelled out in official patch notes either.

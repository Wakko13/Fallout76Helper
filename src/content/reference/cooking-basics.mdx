For what the Hunger and Water meters actually do (and why keeping them full is worth bothering with at all),
see [chems, hunger, thirst, and disease basics](../../faq/chems-and-consumables-basics/) — this entry is about
the cooking *station* mechanic itself, not the meters it feeds.

## Why cook at all

Nearly every raw meat/vegetable you loot or harvest can be eaten as-is, but a Cooking Station (2 Steel + 2 Wood
to build, found pre-placed all over Appalachia too) turns it into something strictly better along three axes:

- **Disease risk drops to (or near) zero.** Raw meat carries a real disease chance — raw Radstag Meat, for
  example, lists a 7% chance of disease when eaten uncooked. The cooked version, Grilled Radstag, lists none.
  This is the main reason cooking matters at all past the very early game: disease is a level-15+ system with
  real debuffs, and raw meat is one of the main ways to catch one (see the FAQ above for the full list of disease
  sources and cures).
- **Radiation exposure goes down.** Raw Radstag Meat adds 15 rad; Grilled Radstag adds 7 for a similar hunger
  return. Cooking doesn't zero out radiation entirely (rads are baked into a lot of wasteland food generally),
  but it roughly halves it on most meat dishes.
- **Nutritional/buff value goes up.** Cooked dishes restore more hunger and HP per item than the raw ingredient
  alone, and only cooked dishes carry the extra magnitude/duration buffs (carry weight, resistances, and — see
  [SPECIAL-boosting foods](../special-boosting-foods/) — temporary SPECIAL stat points). Raw ingredients are
  strictly a worse, riskier version of the same hunger relief.

## How recipes are learned

Two tiers, and it's easy to conflate them with crafting Plans (which are for weapons/armor/CAMP items, not
food) — recipes and plans are separate systems that both get "learned" into your Pip-Boy but are found and used
differently:

- **Basic recipes are learned automatically** the first time you pick up the relevant raw ingredient — grab your
  first Carrot or Radstag Meat and the matching Soup/Grilled recipe just appears at the Cooking Station, no
  separate item needed.
- **Standout/rarer recipes need a dedicated Recipe item** found as world loot, quest rewards, or vendor stock
  before they show up at the station — Deathclaw Wellington (Moonshiner's Shack) is a good example. A handful of
  recipe locations are still undocumented on community wikis even now (Brain Fungus Soup and Mirelurk Softshell
  Cake, for instance, are listed as unknown/"???" origin on Fextralife) — if a recipe you want isn't dropping
  from an obvious source, it may just be a low drop-rate world/vendor item rather than something you're missing
  a quest step for.

## Canned and pre-war food: no cooking needed

Pre-war packaged food (canned goods, Fancy Lads Snack Cakes, Blamco Mac & Cheese, and similar) is a separate
category that never needed cooking in the first place — it doesn't spoil and carries no disease risk by design,
at the cost of smaller hunger restoration and usually a couple of rads per can (Canned Dog Food, for example,
restores only 10 HP for +2 rad). It's a reasonable stopgap when you're out of fresh ingredients or don't want to
detour to a Cooking Station, but it doesn't compete with a cooked meal on nutrition-per-item.

## The "Chef perk" — it doesn't exist in Fallout 76

If you're expecting a dedicated Intelligence "Chef" perk boosting cooking yield or buff duration/magnitude (the
way Fallout 4's Local Leader tree has one), Fallout 76 doesn't have it — there's no perk card by that name, and
no card that directly scales cooked-food buff strength or duration. The closest related cards, none of which
work quite like a "Chef" perk would:

- **Good With Salt** (Intelligence, level 9) — food in your inventory spoils 45%/90% slower per rank. Purely a
  shelf-life perk; doesn't touch cooking output or buffs.
- **Super Duper** (Luck, level 50) — a 10/20/30%-per-rank chance to get double output from *any* craft, cooking
  included, so it's the closest thing to a "cooking yield" perk that exists, but it's not food-specific.
- **Slow Metabolizer** (Endurance, level 5, humans only) — makes all food satisfy 75% more hunger per item
  (its drink-side twin, Dromedary, does the same for thirst), stretching how far your cooked food goes without
  changing what any individual dish does.

## Uncertain / worth double-checking

Exact disease-chance percentages and rad values per raw ingredient are pulled from individual community wiki
item pages rather than a single official table, and Bethesda doesn't publish them directly — treat the
Radstag numbers above as representative of the raw-vs-cooked pattern rather than a guarantee every raw
ingredient follows the same ratio. A few cooking recipe origins are genuinely undocumented by the community
wikis themselves (marked "???"), not just missing from this entry.

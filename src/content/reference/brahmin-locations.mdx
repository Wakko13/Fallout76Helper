Brahmin are the two-headed mutated cattle of the Fallout world, and Appalachia
has two distinct kinds you'll run into:

- **Wild/feral Brahmin** — unowned, roam freely, safe to kill for **Brahmin
  Meat** (used in Ribeye Steak, Appalachili, etc.) and **Brahmin Hide** (breaks
  down into 4x Leather). This entry covers these.
- **Tame/settlement Brahmin** — penned animals belonging to an NPC or
  settlement (Foundation, The Wayward, Anchor Farm). Attacking these has
  consequences: shooting the Brahmin at The Wayward turns the resident Sentry
  Bot "Bessie" hostile, and killing settlement-owned Brahmin generally hurts
  faction standing. If you just want Brahmin Milk, you can milk a tame Brahmin
  without harming it. Leave these alone unless a quest specifically wants you
  to interact with them.

## Best wild Brahmin spots

- **Flatwoods** — up to 4 Brahmin, most reliably near the church in the town
  center.
- **Big Bend Tunnel East** — up to 3 Brahmin, often right at the fast-travel
  point.
- **Pleasant Valley Station** — 3 Brahmin along the shoreline of the nearby
  lake.
- **Middle Mountain Pitstop** — around 4 Brahmin.
- **Lewis & Sons Farming Supply** — 1 Brahmin, behind a building.

## Random encounters

- The **Brahmin Wrangler** random encounter features 3 Brahmin traveling with
  the NPC Mr. Herder.
- Public events **Free Range**, **Riding Shotgun**, and **Spin the Wheel** can
  also spawn Brahmin.

## Uncertain / worth double-checking

Spawn counts (Flatwoods' "up to 4," Big Bend Tunnel's "up to 3") come from
community location tallies rather than an official spawn table and can vary
between visits. Whether a given Brahmin counts as "wild" versus "owned by a
nearby settlement" isn't always visually obvious at a glance — if it's
standing in or right next to a fenced pen with NPCs around, treat it as tame
and skip it unless you've confirmed otherwise.

[CAMP building gotchas](/faq/camp-building-gotchas) — this entry covers the underlying mechanics instead.

## Budget: how it's actually calculated

Your CAMP has a single build meter shown as a percentage/bar in Build Mode. It is **fixed** — there's no known
way to permanently increase it (leveling up does not expand it, despite some claims floating around; treat
those as unconfirmed). Once the meter fills, you can't place more items even if you have the raw materials for
them, and you must scrap something first.

- Every placeable object has a **budget cost** driven mainly by its complexity — roughly its polygon count and
  how much is animated/interactive. A flat wall or floor piece costs very little; a turret, generator, crafting
  bench, or animated decoration costs a lot more for the same "one item" placed.
- The community-run **Flamingo Unit** system (named after the Lawn Flamingo decoration, which costs almost
  exactly 1 unit) is the closest thing to a documented cost table, since Bethesda doesn't publish exact numbers.
  Under that system: a CAMP tops out around **500 units**, a claimed Workshop around **1,500 units**, and
  Shelters have their own separate budget entirely (see below). Most basic structural pieces cost around 1 unit;
  a wire connection costs a fraction of a unit (~0.25); turrets, generators, and displays cost several times
  that.
- **Items in your Storage tab still count against budget**, whether or not they're actually placed in the
  world. Blueprints you've saved also count against budget while stored.
- There is no separate player-facing "complexity" meter distinct from budget — complexity is the *input* that
  determines how much budget an individual item costs, not a second pool you manage independently. If you see
  the term used elsewhere, it almost always means this same underlying budget cost.

## Shelters have their own separate budget

CAMP Shelters (the instanced Vault-style interior spaces: Utility Room, Lobby, Atrium) are not part of your main
CAMP's budget at all — each shelter type has its own build budget, generally larger than your main CAMP's. You
can own one of each shelter type at a time, build multiple entrances to the same shelter, and shelter interiors
persist independently even if you move your CAMP or destroy the entrance.

## Blueprint (aka camp plan)

A blueprint is a saved snapshot of some or all of your build that you can place again later, including on a
different plot or after a CAMP move:

1. In Build Mode, switch to the Blueprint/Modify tool (TAB on PC) and hold the select key (E) to start tagging
   items.
2. Select everything you want included — the UI shows a running "blueprint budget" as you go.
3. Confirm (R) to save it as a blueprint.

Two things to know about placing one back down:

- Blueprints save to your build menu's **Blueprint** category. Placing from there **re-crafts every item from
  raw materials** — expensive if the blueprint is large.
- When you use **Move CAMP**, the game automatically bundles your current build into the **Stored** category
  instead (not Blueprint) — placing from Stored uses the copies you already built, at no extra material cost,
  but is pickier about needing matching terrain.

There's no official published cap on how many blueprints you can save; in practice the limiting factor is that
saved blueprints count toward your CAMP budget while stored, so a handful of large ones can eat your budget
before you've placed anything.

## Multiple CAMP slots and moving

- Every character gets **2 free CAMP slots** by default (your original CAMP plus one more). Up to **8
  additional slots** (10 total) can be purchased from the Atomic Shop.
- Only **one CAMP can be active/placed in the world at a time**, even with multiple slots owned — switch which
  one is active from the Map or the CAMP Slots menu ("Activate CAMP"), and the others sit dormant until you
  switch back.
- **Moving your CAMP is not free** — the Pip-Boy "Move" option charges a caps fee that scales with character
  level (roughly in the 15-40+ caps range at time of writing), separate from any material cost of rebuilding.
- If someone else is occupying your saved CAMP location when you place it (e.g. after a server switch), the
  game notifies you and lets you place elsewhere for free rather than forcing a paid move.

## Uncertain / worth double-checking

Exact Flamingo Unit costs and the 500/1,500-unit budget ceilings come from community spreadsheets, not an
official Bethesda number — treat them as "the right order of magnitude." The caps cost to move a CAMP scaling
with level is consistently reported by players but isn't in official documentation either. Blueprint slot
limits, if any exist, aren't documented anywhere found — the practical constraint is budget consumption, not a
hard count.
